Furosemide
Pharmacology
Loop diuretic
Telemetry
Educational reference — not clinical decision support
In short: Helps the body get rid of extra fluid by making the kidneys release more sodium and water — commonly used when fluid buildup makes it hard to breathe or puts strain on the heart.
Nursing implications: Monitor potassium and renal function, watch for orthostatic hypotension, track daily weights and I&Os, assess for ototoxicity with rapid IV push.
Side effects / adverse effects: Dehydration, hypokalemia, hypotension/dizziness, increased urination, and — less commonly — tinnitus or hearing changes with rapid IV administration.
Common brand name: Lasix. Usually given IV push or PO depending on acuity and how quickly fluid needs to come off.
Related concepts
CHF Fluid balance Potassium wasting Loop diuretics (class) Daily weights & I&Os Ototoxicity
