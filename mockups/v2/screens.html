Furosemide
Pharmacology
Loop diuretic
Telemetry
Educational reference — not clinical decision support
In short: Helps the body get rid of extra fluid by making the kidneys release more sodium and water — commonly used when fluid buildup makes it hard to breathe or puts strain on the heart.
Nursing implications: Monitor potassium and renal function, watch for orthostatic hypotension, track daily weights and I&Os, assess for ototoxicity with rapid IV push.
Common brand name: Lasix. Usually given IV push or PO depending on acuity and how quickly fluid needs to come off.

Furosemide
Why: treats fluid overload from CHF exacerbation
Helps your body get rid of extra fluid that's making it hard to breathe.
Metoprolol
Why: controls heart rate & reduces cardiac workload
Slows and steadies the heartbeat so the heart doesn't have to work as hard.
Lisinopril
Why: lowers blood pressure & eases strain on the heart
Relaxes blood vessels to lower blood pressure and take pressure off the heart.
Potassium chloride
Why: replaces potassium lost from diuretic therapy
Furosemide causes the body to lose potassium along with fluid, so this tops it back up to a safe level.
